In brief

Who is Dr. Moses Adoko?

Dr. Moses Adoko is the former Chief Knowledge Officer of NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center, the Center that operates the Hubble Space Telescope and managed the James Webb. He spent more than 25 years at NASA in leadership, management and technical roles.

As Chief Knowledge Officer, his job was to make sure that what engineers, scientists and project managers learned reached the next team, the next project and the next mission. He is an expert and thought leader on why mega and complex projects overrun, backed by published research.

He is Founder & CEO of MakAstro Tech & Services, USA, and lectures at Columbia University. Today he works with organizations across Mauritius in partnership with Innovanta One, and across Africa through MakAstro Tech & Services.

Dr. Adoko is one of the few architects of NASA’s strategic and institutional knowledge management function. He now works with a small number of organizations in Mauritius and Africa.

The problem

What walks out your door

Four situations every Chief Executive Officer will recognize.

01

The engineer who knew why

Your most experienced people are retiring or emigrating. The systems they ran stay behind. The reasons behind a thousand decisions leave with them — and no handover document holds twenty years of judgement and technical reasoning.

02

The project that forgot

Your last big program or project ran late and over budget. A review was written, filed and never read. The next one is now quietly repeating the same mistakes, at a larger scale.

03

The same lesson, bought twice

Two teams in the same organization hit the same problem a year apart. Each solved it from scratch. You paid for that lesson twice — and you will pay again, because neither team knows the other learned it.

04

AI fed on thin knowledge

You are investing in AI. But AI is only as good as the knowledge you feed it — and most of what your organization knows sits in people’s heads, not in any system an AI can reach.

NASA cannot afford to forget. Neither can you.

What follows is the career of the man whose job, at NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center, was to make sure of exactly that.

The NASA story

From technical curiosity to NASA Goddard’s Chief Knowledge Officer

Dr. Moses Adoko’s path began with technical curiosity and led to the top of NASA Goddard’s knowledge management function.

As Chief Knowledge Officer of NASA’s Goddard Space Flight Center — the Center that operates the Hubble Space Telescope and managed the James Webb — he was responsible for how one of the world’s most demanding science, engineering and project management organizations learns: how it identifies, captures and applies the critical knowledge behind institutional growth and mission success.

He helped develop the policies that govern how NASA does systems engineering and manages critical knowledge for mission success. He taught NASA’s engineers, scientists and people in allied technical disciplines, and ran multiple learning programs for NASA.

He managed the NASA Goddard Senior Fellows Program: senior scientists and engineers who advise the Center Director and leadership on science, engineering, technology, research investment and strategy.

In 2020, NASA headquarters called him up as Deputy Chief Knowledge Officer for the entire agency. He then returned to Goddard as its Chief Knowledge Officer.

The methods

How NASA keeps what it knows

Six practices Dr. Adoko led, developed, instituted and strengthened at Goddard — each built so that hard-won lessons change the next decision, not just fill an archive.

1

Lessons learned that get used

Not a database nobody opens. Lessons are captured in the flow of work and routed to the teams making the same decisions next.

2

Pause and learn

Short, structured reflection sessions inside live projects — so teams correct course mid-flight instead of at the post-mortem.

3

Critical-knowledge capture

Before a senior expert retires, what only they know is identified and transferred. The person leaves; the judgement stays.

4

Formal dissent channels

A protected process for employees to say “different routes are needed” — so that difficult news reaches decision-makers while it is still cheap to act on.

5

A Council of Senior Experts

Goddard’s Senior Fellows advise the Center Director on strategy, investment and risk. Dr. Adoko managed and administered how that expertise reached decisions.

6

Knowledge workshops

He led sessions to capture and share critical knowledge with key functional units, programs and projects, and with the teams behind the James Webb Space Telescope, the L’Ralph instrument on Lucy, OSIRIS-REx, TESS and the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter, among many others — turning mission experience into shared institutional practice.

His mandate as CKO, in NASA’s words: to ensure the Center “operates as a learning organization in support of mission success”.

The same discipline, applied to your organization, is what the next sections describe.

Your succession plan names the successor. It does not make them ready.

Training alone does not make a leader, or build the competencies and proficiency the role needs. This program couples training with structured knowledge transfer and the right culture, so your next leader is fully proficient before the handover — not after it.

Leadership, managerial, technical and professional training that responds to your current and future needs.
